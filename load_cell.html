Project Overview

This is probably the most challenging project I have worked on so far. I had to teach my self mechanical engineering concepts, convince the machine shop manager to let me use the mill, and did countless FEA simulations to ensure the load cell would not deform under my desired load of 400Lbs, while keeping the measurment points on the arms weak enough so that they would experience stress and strain that was measurable. Only after this did I decide to mill the load cell out of 6061 aluminum.

I used a full bridge strain gauge configuration to measure the stress and strain in the 4 most sensitive parts of each axis. I scribed the positions for the strain gauges so that they were in identical position on both sides, and adhered them with cyanoacrylate glue. I then soldered the wires to the strain gauges and ran the wiring through small holes in the load cell to keep everything tidy.

For the electronics, I decided to use an HX711 chip to measure each axis. This chip is a 24 bit ADC designed to measure strain gauges. I used a separate HX711 for each axis, and used an ESP32 to read the data from each HX711, and send it to my computer over usb.

To account for cell crosstalk, I had to calibrate the load cell by applying a 10kg load in exactly the X, Y, and Z directions, and recording the output of each axis. I used this data to create a correction matrix that is multiplied by the load cell output to remove the crosstalk error.

I used a java program to read the data and graph the force vector in a 3D space along with the forces in each direction, and the total magnitude of the force vector.

The final product was a fully functional load cell that could measure and distinguish forces in 3 axes with a Maximum force before deformation of 400lbs.
